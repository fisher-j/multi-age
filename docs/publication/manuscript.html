Regeneration and fuel loading with varying overstory retention in redwood stands

Working publication manuscript structure

Author

Judson Fisher

Published

September 11, 2026

Abstract

Forest managers using retention forestry need information on regeneration and fuel development to design productive stands in fire-adapted ecosystems. We analyzed 10-year remeasurement data from the Redwood Multiaged Experiment in Jackson Demonstration State Forest, California. The experiment compared group selection, aggregated retention, and low- and high-density dispersed retention treatments and measured regeneration, sprout growth, and surface fuel loading.

Redwood regeneration responded strongly to increasing canopy openness, whereas tanoak responses were more modest. Fuel measurements describe conditions before and after precommercial thinning and provide reference values for future fire and fuels modeling in these redwood systems. The manuscript will evaluate how overstory retention affects regeneration while presenting fuel components in a form useful for integrated silviculture and fire management.

Introduction

Developing and maintaining multiaged stand structures requires silvicultural systems that create and sustain cohorts of different ages within the same stand (O’Hara, 1998; Schütz, 2002). Retention systems can retain a range of tree densities in dispersed or aggregated patterns and can be used to maintain multiaged stands or transform formerly even-aged stands (Mitchell & Beese, 2002).

Coast redwood (Sequoia sempervirens) is well suited to this objective because it regenerates reliably by stump sprouting and can grow beneath a retained overstory. Its response to light is nevertheless important: reduced access to light can limit sprout vigor and persistence (Berrill et al., 2018; Muma et al., 2022; O’Hara et al., 2007). Tanoak (Notholithocarpus densiflorus) is also shade tolerant and a vigorous resprouter, making its response relevant to both stand development and management of competing vegetation (Berrill et al., 2021; O’Hara et al., 2017).

Silvicultural decisions that promote regeneration also influence the fuel conditions in which later management and fire occur. This paper therefore examines regeneration under varying overstory retention and describes surface fuel conditions as reference information for future fire-modeling and management studies.

We use 10-year measurements from the Redwood Multiaged Experiment to evaluate regeneration responses among retention treatments and to report fuel conditions before and after precommercial thinning. The regeneration analysis provides the primary basis for inference; fuel components will be presented in a publication-specific form after their analytical specification is reviewed.

Methods

Study area and design

The experiment is located in the Jackson Demonstration State Forest in coastal northern California. Four harvest treatments were replicated at four sites in 2012: group selection (GS), high-density dispersed retention (HD), high-density aggregated retention (HA), and low-density dispersed retention (LD). The retained-overstory treatments targeted post-harvest relative densities of 13% or 21%; group selection created a 1-ha opening with a lightly thinned buffer. These treatments were designed to examine alternative pathways for developing multiaged redwood stands (Berrill & O’Hara, 2009; Reineke, 1933).

Ten years after harvest, regeneration plots and fuel transects were established within each 0.2-ha treatment macro plot. Regeneration measurements characterized redwood, tanoak, and Douglas-fir; fuel transects characterized dead surface fuels and live vegetation. The plots were remeasured after precommercial thinning, which released selected conifers from competing trees and shrubs.

Results

Regeneration under varying overstory retention

The thesis results provide the starting material for basal area, Douglas-fir occurrence, and sprout-height summaries. At 10 years after harvest, redwood regeneration was greatest in the most open treatment and declined with greater overstory retention. Tanoak responses were comparatively modest, and Douglas-fir regeneration was less frequent. The final figures and summaries will retain these results only after the publication presentation and modeling review are complete.

Fuel conditions as reference values

Fuel conditions are reported second to support interpretation of the silvicultural treatments and to provide reference values for subsequent fire and fuels studies. The thesis found comparatively few treatment differences in fine dead fuels before precommercial thinning, whereas vegetation fuels were greater in more open treatments. The publication analysis will separate fuel components that were previously combined and will limit treatment-comparison emphasis to findings that aid interpretation.

Discussion

The discussion will first interpret regeneration responses in relation to the silvicultural objective of developing a new redwood cohort beneath varying levels of retained overstory. It will then explain how the fuel dataset complements that objective by characterizing conditions that others can use in fire and fuels work.

This structure treats silviculture and fire management as linked management questions while keeping conclusions within the support of the reviewed analyses and cited literature.
